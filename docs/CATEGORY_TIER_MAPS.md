# Category list and tier maps

Written 2026-08-20. The category list was the item blocking authoring in `docs/HANDOFF.md`.
It is now settled: **29 categories x 9 tiers x 20 questions = 5,220 questions.**

Read this together with `docs/SOURCES.md`, which records where the text comes from.

## Why this document exists

The handoff's rubric defines the nine tiers in fiqh vocabulary: a ruling and its condition
(4), a narration with its grading (5), context of revelation (6), where the schools differ
(8). That maps cleanly onto Fiqh, Five Pillars and Finance. It does not map onto Islamic
History, Sacred Geography, Arts & Culture, Science in Islam or Arabic Language — **over half
the list.**

Forcing it produces exactly failure mode #3: tier-8 labels on tier-3 facts in harder
vocabulary. So the rubric is kept, but read at its real altitude:

| tier | rank | the fiqh reading | **what it is actually asking** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mubtadi | everyday fact | a single fact a practising child would know |
| 2 | Talib | maktab teaching | still single-fact recall |
| 3 | Hafiz | names, numbers, sequences | requires having actually studied |
| 4 | Faqih | a ruling **and its condition** | **a claim and the condition that qualifies it** |
| 5 | Muhaddith | narration with source or grading | **the provenance of the claim — who reports it, how reliably** |
| 6 | Mufassir | context of revelation | **context that changes what the thing means** |
| 7 | Shaykh | two positions, or an exception | comparing, or knowing where the general rule stops |
| 8 | Imam | where the schools differ **and why** | **where the authorities disagree and what method separates them** |
| 9 | Mujaddid | synthesis | applying a principle to a case, or a chain of reasoning |

Tier 5 generalises to **provenance**; tier 8 generalises to **disagreement among
authorities**. Every category below has both. None needed padding to get there.

**Difficulty is not obscurity.** Every line below was written to demand a different *kind*
of knowing, not a rarer fact. Check each finished bucket against its line here before moving
on; a question that does not match its line is rewritten, not relabelled.

## Order

Ordered by **dependency, not difficulty** — each band assumes the ones above it. The banding
is also a mobile concern: 29 flat entries is a long scroll at 380px.

# Band 1 — Foundations

## 1. Creed (Aqeedah)

1. The six articles of faith, by name.
2. What tawhid means; that Allah has no partner; that a prophet is not divine.
3. The categories of tawhid as the scholars name them, and the articles in order.
4. A belief and what makes it valid — what iman requires beyond verbal statement.
5. The verse or hadith a creedal point rests on, and where it is recorded.
6. What a term meant in its Qur'anic context versus how it is used now (fitrah, kufr).
7. An exception to a general creedal rule — when ignorance excuses; an act of kufr versus takfir of a person.
8. Where Ash'ari, Maturidi and Athari readings differ on the divine attributes, and what method separates them.
9. Apply a creedal principle to a case the classical texts never addressed.

## 2. Allah's Names & Attributes

1. That Allah is Ar-Rahman and Ar-Rahim, and that these open almost every surah.
2. The meanings of the most commonly recited names.
3. Where a given name occurs in the Qur'an, by surah.
4. A name and the condition on its use — names used only of Allah versus those a person may bear (Karim, Rashid), and what makes the difference.
5. **The provenance problem, which this category must teach rather than hide:** the *statement* that Allah has 99 names is sahih (Tirmidhi 3506, 3508); the *enumeration* of them (Tirmidhi 3507) is graded **Daif by Ahmad Shakir, Al-Albani and Zubair Ali Zai alike**.
6. What an attribute means in Qur'anic usage versus its everyday Arabic sense.
7. Two names that appear to pull against each other (Al-Qabid / Al-Basit) and how the scholars hold them together.
8. Where the schools differ on the attributes — tafwid, ta'wil, ithbat — and the method behind each.
9. Derive from a name a consequence for how a person should act, and defend the derivation.

> **Authoring constraint.** Anchor every name to its Qur'anic occurrence, never to the
> Tirmidhi list. Never ask "is X one of the 99?" as a question of fact — the list itself is
> not established. The discrepancy makes an excellent tier 9.

## 3. Five Pillars

1. Name the five pillars.
2. Number of daily prayers; the month of fasting; the direction of prayer.
3. Rak'ah counts, nisab thresholds, the sequence of the hajj rites.
4. A ruling and its condition — when zakat falls due; what breaks a fast versus what merely diminishes it.
5. The hadith a specific practice rests on, with its collection and grading.
6. Why a ruling came when it did — the staged prohibition, the change of qiblah.
7. An exception: the traveller, the ill, the one who forgets — what shifts and what does not.
8. Where the four schools differ on performance (hand position, the status of witr, zakat on debt) and each school's basis.
9. Apply the rules to a case classical fiqh never faced — zakat on crypto holdings; fasting where the sun does not set.

## 4. Holy Quran

1. That the Qur'an is Allah's word revealed to Muhammad (ﷺ); the name of the first surah.
2. The number of surahs; the longest and the shortest; what Makkan and Madinan mean.
3. Juz boundaries, surah order, which surah holds a given famous verse.
4. A ruling attached to the text and its condition — the sajdah of recitation; touching the mushaf.
5. Which reports establish a surah's virtue, and how they are graded.
6. Why a passage was revealed, and how knowing that changes the reading.
7. Two verses that appear to conflict, and how the scholars reconcile them.
8. Where scholars differ on a verse's ruling status — claims of abrogation, and who rejects them.
9. Trace a theme across surahs and show what the arrangement itself teaches.

## 5. Prophetic Biography (Seerah)

1. His name, his parents, where he was born.
2. The cave, the first revelation, the migration to Madinah.
3. Dates and sequence of the major expeditions; ages at key events.
4. A practice he established, and the circumstance that conditioned it.
5. Which sira author or collection reports an incident — Ibn Ishaq via Ibn Hisham, al-Waqidi, Bukhari — and how their reliability differs.
6. The context behind a decision that looks strange without it — the terms at Hudaybiyyah.
7. Two accounts of one event differing in detail, and which is better supported.
8. Where the sira sources conflict on chronology, and why historians prefer one.
9. Draw a principle of leadership or forbearance from an episode and apply it to a modern case.

# Band 2 — The sources, and how we read them

## 6. Hadith Sciences

1. What a hadith is; that they are not all equally reliable.
2. The names of the six major collections.
3. Definitions: sahih, hasan, da'if, mawdu', mutawatir, ahad.
4. The conditions of a sahih hadith, and what fails when one is missing.
5. **The da'if format's home tier** — given four real narrations, identify the weak one from its grading.
6. What a technical term means precisely — mursal, mu'allaq, mawquf, maqtu' — and why the distinction decides cases.
7. Two graders differing on one hadith, and the reasoning on each side.
8. Where the schools differ on accepting a mursal or a solitary report as evidence, and why.
9. Given a chain and its narrators, reason toward a grading and justify it.

## 7. Quran Commentary (Tafsir)

1. That tafsir means explaining the Qur'an, not translating it.
2. The names of the best-known mufassirun.
3. Which mufassir wrote which work, and roughly when.
4. A rule of interpretation and its condition — when a verse is read literally.
5. Where a tafsir claim comes from: the Prophet (ﷺ), a Companion, or the mufassir's own reasoning.
6. Asbab al-nuzul — the occasion of revelation, and what it settles.
7. Tafsir bi'l-ma'thur against bi'l-ra'y on the same verse.
8. Where the mufassirun genuinely differ on a meaning, and what each relies on.
9. Apply an accepted interpretive principle to a verse and defend the reading.

## 8. Preservation of the Qur'an (Jam' & Qira'at)

1. That the Qur'an was memorised and written down in the Prophet's (ﷺ) lifetime.
2. That Abu Bakr gathered it and 'Uthman standardised it.
3. Who the scribes were; the sequence of the two compilations; what the 'Uthmanic mushaf did to earlier copies.
4. What makes a recitation canonical — the three conditions — and what fails without them.
5. Which reports establish the compilation account, and where they are recorded.
6. The seven ahruf: what the term meant, and why it is not the same as the qira'at.
7. Hafs and Warsh on the same word, and what actually differs.
8. Where scholars differ on the meaning of the ahruf and on the status of the shadhdh readings.
9. Given a variant reading, explain what it does and does not change about the meaning.

## 9. Tajwid

1. That the Qur'an is recited by rules, not read like a newspaper.
2. Isti'adhah and basmalah; what a madd is, at its simplest.
3. The articulation points; the rules of noon sakinah and tanwin, by name.
4. A rule and its condition — when qalqalah is greater; when a madd lengthens.
5. Where the rules come from — the chains of recitation and the ijazah system.
6. What a term means technically rather than loosely — ghunnah, ikhfa', idgham with and without ghunnah.
7. Two rules that collide in a single word, and which governs.
8. Where the transmissions differ — Hafs, Warsh, Qalun on madd counts — and why.
9. Given an unfamiliar verse, identify every rule operating in it and justify each.

## 10. Arabic Language

1. The alphabet; that Arabic is written right to left.
2. Common Qur'anic words; singular and plural; masculine and feminine.
3. Root systems, verb forms, the ten measures by number.
4. A grammatical rule and its condition — when a noun takes nasb.
5. Which grammarian or school established a rule — Basran or Kufan.
6. How i'rab changes the meaning of a specific verse.
7. Two parsings of one verse, and what each yields.
8. Where the Basran and Kufan grammarians differ, and what method separates them.
9. Parse an unfamiliar verse and show how the parsing constrains its interpretation.

> `corpus.quran.com` serves full syntactic dependency treebanks and a root-based Qur'anic
> dictionary (both verified live). Tiers 6–9 here are built on that, not on rarer vocabulary.

# Band 3 — Living it

## 11. Usul al-Fiqh

1. That scholars derive rulings from the Qur'an and Sunnah rather than inventing them.
2. The four agreed sources, by name.
3. The five ahkam; the difference between fard and wajib in Hanafi usage.
4. A source and the condition on using it — when qiyas is valid; what an 'illah must satisfy.
5. Where a legal maxim comes from, and which work records it.
6. What a term means technically — 'amm, khass, mutlaq, muqayyad — and why the difference decides cases.
7. Two sources conflicting on one question, and the order of precedence.
8. Where the schools differ on the disputed sources — istihsan, masalih mursalah, 'amal ahl al-Madinah — and why.
9. Apply a legal maxim to a case with no precedent, and defend the derivation.

> This category is why the others can have a real tier 8. It teaches the reasoning the rest
> of the app otherwise only shows the output of.

## 12. Islamic Law (Fiqh)

1. Halal and haram; that some things are obligatory and some forbidden.
2. What breaks wudu; what makes food halal.
3. The chapters of fiqh in order; the schools and their founders, with dates.
4. A ruling and its condition — najasah thresholds; the conditions of a valid sale.
5. The hadith a ruling rests on, and its grading.
6. Why a ruling exists — the maslahah it protects.
7. An exception: necessity and hardship, and where they stop.
8. Where the four schools differ on a practical question, and each one's evidence.
9. Rule on a novel case and show the reasoning.

## 13. Islamic Ethics (Akhlaq)

1. Truthfulness; kindness to parents; not lying.
2. Named virtues and vices; the rights of a neighbour.
3. The sins of the tongue distinguished precisely — ghibah, namimah, buhtan.
4. A virtue and its condition — when concealing a fault is required, and when disclosure is.
5. The narration behind an ethical teaching, and its grading.
6. What a term meant then against now — hilm, muru'ah, haya'.
7. Two duties in conflict — honesty against protecting someone — and how the scholars order them.
8. Where scholars differ on contested cases: permissible lying; backbiting the wrongdoer.
9. Resolve an ethical dilemma with no clear precedent, using established principles.

## 14. Du'a & Dhikr

1. Bismillah; alhamdulillah; the du'a before eating and before sleeping.
2. The common adhkar, and when they are said.
3. The wording of the well-known supplications, exactly; the morning and evening counts.
4. An adab of du'a and its condition — facing the qiblah, raising the hands, and when not to.
5. **Unusually rich grading territory** — which widely circulated supplications are weak or fabricated, and who graded them so.
6. What a phrase inside a supplication actually means.
7. Two reports of one dhikr differing in wording or count, and which is stronger.
8. Where scholars differ — collective dhikr, raising the hands after fard prayer — and why.
9. Judge an unfamiliar supplication against the criteria and justify the judgement.

## 15. Sufism & Spirituality

1. That Islam asks for sincerity of the heart, not outward acts alone.
2. Ikhlas, taqwa, tawakkul, sabr — what they mean.
3. Named figures and their works — al-Qushayri's Risalah, al-Ghazali's Ihya.
4. A spiritual practice and its condition — what makes dhikr sound rather than innovated.
5. Where a teaching comes from — Qur'an, sound hadith, or a master's own words.
6. What a technical term means — maqam, hal, the stages of the nafs.
7. Two approaches to one discipline, and what separates them.
8. **Where Sunni scholars genuinely differ on tasawwuf itself** — accepted, qualified, or rejected — with each position stated as its holders would state it.
9. Assess a spiritual claim or practice against the Qur'an and Sunnah, and defend the verdict.

> **Measured:** only 33 hadith in the graded corpus match this category's vocabulary, and
> 28% of their grade-assertions are weak — **1.61x the 17% baseline**, the highest of any
> category tested. Anchor this category in classical texts (Ihya, Risalah — both on
> archive.org), not in narration. Tier 8 must be handled without partisanship.

## 16. Afterlife (Akhirah)

1. That there is life after death, a Day of Judgement, Paradise and Hell.
2. The questioning in the grave; the scale; the bridge.
3. The names and levels of Paradise and Hell; the order of events on that Day.
4. A belief and its condition — who enters without reckoning, and on what basis.
5. Which narration describes a specific scene, and its grading — much popular material here is weak.
6. Whether a described scene is read literally or figuratively by the mufassirun.
7. Two reports differing on sequence, and how they are reconciled.
8. Where scholars differ — the nature of the punishment of the grave; the eternity of Hell — and why.
9. Reason from established principles about what the texts leave unspecified.

# Band 4 — The people

## 17. Companions (Sahaba)

1. Abu Bakr, 'Umar, 'Uthman, 'Ali — who they were.
2. The first Muslims; the rightly-guided caliphs, in order.
3. Kunyas, tribal affiliations, who narrated most, who died when.
4. A Companion's status and its basis — what makes someone a sahabi at all.
5. Which collection records a stated virtue, and how it is graded.
6. The context of an incident that changes how it reads.
7. Two Companions differing on a question in the Prophet's (ﷺ) lifetime, and how it resolved.
8. Where the historians differ on the fitnah, and what each relies on.
9. Draw a principle from a Companion's conduct and apply it.

> Tier 8 is the hardest bucket in this category and must be authored without partisanship:
> report where the sources conflict, do not adjudicate between Companions.

## 18. Ahl al-Bayt

1. Fatimah, Hasan, Husayn, Khadijah — who they were.
2. The Prophet's (ﷺ) wives and children, by name.
3. Genealogy, dates, who descends from whom.
4. Who counts as Ahl al-Bayt, and on what basis — the scope is itself disputed; state the condition.
5. Which narration establishes a virtue, and its grading.
6. The context of the verse of purification and the hadith of the cloak.
7. Two Sunni readings of the same text, and what separates them.
8. Where **Sunni** scholars differ on the term's scope — noting plainly that other traditions read it differently, without arguing against them.
9. Reason about the family's status from established Sunni principles.

> The Sunni condition from the handoff binds hardest here. Describe other traditions
> accurately where they are relevant; never polemicise, and never present a Sunni position
> as though no other reading existed.

## 19. Other Prophets

1. Adam, Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, 'Isa — names, and one fact each.
2. The 25 named in the Qur'an; who received which scripture.
3. Lineages, sequence, which surah tells which story.
4. What a prophet's mission required, and what conditioned his people's response.
5. **Whether a detail is Qur'anic, from sound hadith, or isra'iliyyat** — the governing distinction of this category.
6. Why a story sits where it does, and what the surrounding passage uses it for.
7. Two Qur'anic tellings of one story differing in emphasis, and what each adds.
8. Where the mufassirun differ on a detail, and how each treats isra'iliyyat.
9. Extract the principle a narrative is built to convey, and defend the reading.

## 20. Miracles & Signs

1. That Allah gave the prophets signs, and that the Qur'an is the greatest of them.
2. The splitting of the moon; the night journey; the staff of Musa.
3. Which prophet was given which sign, and where each is mentioned.
4. What distinguishes a mu'jizah from a karamah, and the conditions of each.
5. **This category's whole discipline sits here** — is the narration sound, and who says so.
6. Whether the Qur'anic wording describes a physical event or a sign, in the mufassirun's reading.
7. Two accounts of one miracle, and which is better supported.
8. Where scholars differ on the karamat of the awliya, and on how miracles are interpreted.
9. Assess an unfamiliar miracle claim against the criteria and defend the verdict.

> **Could not be measured.** Miracle narrations sit overwhelmingly in Bukhari and Muslim,
> which carry no gradings in the corpus, so the weak-density test returns nothing. That is
> not a clean result — it is no result. This is the category where fabricated material
> circulates most freely in popular use, so it needs the tightest citation discipline of the
> 29. The handoff's rule binds absolutely here: **a weak narration may never be the basis of
> a ruling question — only of a question about the narration itself.**

## 21. Women in Islam

1. Khadijah, 'A'ishah, Maryam — who they were.
2. That women hold rights to inheritance, to property, and to consent in marriage.
3. Named scholars, narrators and patrons — how many hadith 'A'ishah narrated; who Fatimah al-Fihri was.
4. A right and its condition — the status of mahr; inheritance shares and when they vary.
5. Which narration establishes a ruling, and its grading — weak material circulates here from every direction.
6. The occasion of revelation behind a verse concerning women.
7. A general rule and its exception — travel; attendance at the masjid.
8. Where the four schools genuinely differ — the necessity of a wali, khul', testimony — and each school's evidence.
9. Apply established principles to a contemporary question the classical texts did not face.

# Band 5 — The civilisation

## 22. Islamic History

1. That Islam began in Makkah and spread; that there were caliphs after the Prophet (ﷺ).
2. Rashidun, Umayyad, Abbasid — order and rough dates.
3. Dates, dynasties, capitals, battles, sequence.
4. An event and its precondition — what made a conquest or a collapse possible.
5. **Which chronicler reports it** — al-Tabari, al-Baladhuri, Ibn al-Athir — and how their reliability and bias differ.
6. The context that makes a decision intelligible rather than arbitrary.
7. Two accounts of one event, and which the historians prefer.
8. Where the primary sources conflict, and what method resolves them.
9. Explain a historical outcome from its causes, and defend the explanation.

## 23. Sacred Geography

1. Makkah, Madinah, the Ka'bah, Masjid al-Aqsa.
2. Which city holds which masjid; the direction of prayer.
3. The hajj sites in ritual order; distances; historic city names.
4. A site's status and its condition — what makes a masjid a haram; the limits of the sacred precinct.
5. Which narration establishes a site's virtue, and its grading.
6. Why a site matters — the event that made it so.
7. Two sites of similar name or function distinguished — Masjid al-Aqsa and the Dome of the Rock.
8. Where scholars differ on visiting, and on the rulings tied to sacred space.
9. Reason from the principles behind sanctity to a case about a site.

## 24. Islamic Arts & Culture

1. That mosques have minarets and calligraphy, and that the Qur'an is beautifully written.
2. Named scripts and famous mosques.
3. Which script, which architect, which dynasty, which period.
4. A design convention and its condition — why the mihrab points where it does.
5. Which manuscript or monument attests a claim, and where it survives.
6. What a motif meant in its own context.
7. Two regional traditions compared — Ottoman against Mughal; Kufic against Naskh.
8. Where scholars genuinely differ on depiction, imagery and music, and what each relies on.
9. Read an unfamiliar work, place it by period and tradition, and give reasons.

## 25. Science in Islam

1. That Muslim scholars studied medicine, the stars and numbers.
2. Ibn Sina, al-Khwarizmi, Ibn al-Haytham — who did what.
3. Works, dates, institutions, and what each contributed.
4. A method and its condition — what Ibn al-Haytham required of an experiment.
5. Which treatise or manuscript records a contribution.
6. What a concept meant in its own scientific context, rather than by modern analogy.
7. Two scholars differing on a scientific question, and how it was settled.
8. Where the tradition disagreed internally — **and where modern "scientific miracle" claims are contested by Muslim scholars themselves.**
9. Trace an idea's transmission and defend the account.

> Tier 8 is this category's honesty tier. I'jaz 'ilmi claims are popular and contested;
> presenting them as established would be the same error as presenting one school's position
> as consensus.

# Band 6 — Applied and contemporary

## 26. Islamic Finance

1. That interest is forbidden and charity encouraged.
2. What riba means; what zakat is due on.
3. Contract names and definitions — murabaha, mudaraba, musharaka, ijara, salam.
4. A contract and its validity conditions — what invalidates a murabaha.
5. Which text or standard governs — the classical source, or AAOIFI for a modern instrument.
6. What gharar means precisely, and why a contract fails for it.
7. Two structures reaching similar ends, one permitted and one not, and the difference.
8. Where the schools and the modern boards differ — tawarruq, conventional insurance, screening thresholds — and why.
9. Judge an unfamiliar financial product and defend the verdict.

## 27. Marriage & Family Life

1. That marriage is encouraged, and that parents are honoured.
2. That marriage requires consent and a mahr; kindness between spouses.
3. The pillars of nikah; the categories of mahram; the 'iddah periods.
4. A right and its condition — maintenance, custody thresholds, the role of the wali.
5. Which narration establishes an etiquette, and its grading.
6. The occasion behind a verse on marriage or divorce.
7. A general rule and its exception — khul' against talaq; reconciliation before separation.
8. Where the four schools differ — the necessity of a wali, triple talaq, custody age — and each one's basis.
9. Apply the principles to a contemporary family case with no direct precedent.

> Renamed from **Intimacy** on 2026-08-20. Marital intimacy and spousal etiquette remain a
> strand within it. **Measured:** material matching that vocabulary runs **22% weak — 1.27x
> the baseline**, so the popular material is disproportionately unsound and tier 5 carries
> real weight here. The broader framing also supplies the clean fiqh (mahr, nikah
> conditions, wali, talaq, custody) that a nine-tier ladder needs without drifting into
> either explicitness or obscurity at the top.

## 28. Interfaith Relations

1. That Islam recognises the earlier prophets and scriptures.
2. Who the Ahl al-Kitab are; that there is no compulsion in religion.
3. Named treaties and letters; the provisions of the Constitution of Madinah.
4. A ruling and its condition — the dhimmah, and what protection required of each side.
5. Which source records a treaty or letter, and how reliable it is.
6. The context of a verse that reads harshly without it.
7. A general principle and its exception — where the texts distinguish between communities.
8. Where scholars differ on contemporary relations, and what each relies on.
9. Apply the principles to a modern pluralist setting and defend the reasoning.

## 29. Contemporary Issues

1. That Muslims face questions the classical books never addressed.
2. Basic positions on widely discussed modern matters.
3. Named contemporary councils, and their published resolutions.
4. A contemporary ruling and its stated condition.
5. **Which council issued it, when, and by what majority** — and that a contemporary fatwa is not ijma'.
6. What a modern term means precisely, before it can be ruled on at all.
7. Two councils differing on the same question.
8. Where contemporary scholars genuinely differ, and what usul each is applying.
9. Reason toward a position on a question no council has yet addressed, and show the derivation.

> **Author this category last.** It is the only one of the 29 that goes stale: resolutions
> are superseded and circumstances move. Every question here needs a dated citation, and the
> category needs re-review on a schedule the other 28 do not.

## Buckets flagged as at risk before authoring begins

Per the handoff: never pad to reach 20. These are the buckets where twenty genuinely
distinct questions may not exist, named now rather than discovered at question 17.

| category | tier | why |
|---|---|---|
| Sufism & Spirituality | 5 | thinnest measured hadith base of the 29 (33 matches) |
| Miracles & Signs | 5 | gradings sparsest exactly where they are most needed |
| Allah's Names & Attributes | 5 | provenance material is narrow — one narration and its critics |
| Tajwid | 9 | synthesis at this level shades toward qira'at, which is category 8's territory |
| Interfaith Relations | 8 | classical disagreement is thinner than the other fiqh categories |
| Contemporary Issues | 5, 6 | no classical provenance to point at; the tier is reinterpreted, not native |

If any of these cannot sustain twenty distinct questions, the bucket is reported short with
its number — not filled.
